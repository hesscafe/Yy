# Marketing: Hess Cafe

Built from `panel/results.md` and `panel-v2/results.md` (20 simulated buyers), `competitors.md`, `offer.md` and `pricing.md`. There is no launch date yet, so the calendar uses **L = opening day**: L−14 is two weeks before, L+16 is 16 days after.

## Positioning

1. **(Recommended)** For West Village locals and weekend visitors who want a matcha worth the walk (and the photo), Hess Cafe is the morning matcha bar at the Christopher St subway exit that serves dessert-flavoured matcha from 6am. Unlike Matchaful, it opens two and a half hours earlier and costs about $5 less.
   *Evidence:* residents bought 5 of 6, trend chasers 4 of 4, matcha fans 3 of 3. No nearby shop sells dessert matcha before 8:30am (`competitors.md`).
2. For 1-train commuters who need a coffee fast, Hess Cafe is the stop right at the station exit, with a $4 Americano and good matcha you can taste free before you pay.
   *Evidence:* commuters are the weakest segment (3 of 7 even after the new offer). Use this as a secondary message, not the lead.
3. For people who want a treat, Hess Cafe is a tiny pop-up of "dessert you can drink", with cheesecake, cherry pie and apple strudel lattes and torched-meringue hot chocolate.
   *Risk:* it leans into "too sweet", the doubt buyers raised most after quality.

**Not doing yet:** paid search (too few searches for a tiny place), flyers in mailboxes (low return), paid creators (no budget). Optional: invite 2–3 local West Village food creators for a free drink in week 1. If they post, it must be disclosed as a gifted drink.

## Ten hooks

1. "Your matcha, two and a half hours before Matchaful opens." *(convenience; no-name version for ads: "Matcha from 6am at the Christopher St exit.")*
2. "Taste it before you pay. Free matcha sips till 9." *(answers "is the matcha good?")*
3. "We put the farm on the menu." *(quality doubt)*
4. "Cheesecake matcha, half-sweet if you like." *(too sweet)*
5. "Yes, we're open. Coffee inside, 6am to noon." *(window sign; "it looks like a closed bar")*
6. Video, first 3 seconds: the torch hitting the meringue. *(photo buyers)*
7. "$4 Americano. Out in two minutes. Train's not waiting." *(commuters, no drip)*
8. "Don't love it? We remake it. Free." *(trust, no reviews yet)*
9. "10th drink's on us." *(habit: give regulars a reason to switch)*
10. "This week only: [drink of the week]." *(real urgency, changes weekly)*

## The most you can pay to win a customer

- Each drink leaves about **$3.12** on average (`pricing.md` / the cost sheet).
- In the re-test, buyers said they'd buy about **2.4 times in the first month** (31 purchases ÷ 13 buyers).
- So a new customer is worth about **$3.49 × 2.4 ≈ $8.40** in month one, before income tax (mixed menu, `cfo.md`).
- **Spend no more than about $5 to win one customer** for now. That's why the plan uses free drinks (costing about $1 in ingredients) and the window, not paid ads.

## Three numbers to watch every week

| Number | Change something if… |
|---|---|
| Drinks per day (weekday and weekend counted separately) | Weekdays stay under 30 by week 2: push the commuter hooks (#7, #2) and the window |
| Share of buyers who are new vs. punch-card returns | Fewer than 1 in 4 is a return by week 3: the drinks or speed need work |
| Google reviews | Fewer than 10 by L+14: ask every happy customer, every day |

These numbers come from simulated buyers and estimates. Replace them with your real counts from the first two weeks.
