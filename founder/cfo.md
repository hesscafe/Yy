# CFO note: Hess Cafe (main plan: mixed menu)

> **Built on a guess: 60 drinks a day** (the founder's estimate, not yet tested). The real number comes from the 2-week test (Nov 30 – Dec 12). The ramp (30 → 60 over 6 months) is also an estimate. Earlier runs: current prices in `cfo-v1-current-prices.md`, +$1 on everything in `cfo-plus1.md`, lattes at $8 in `cfo-v2-latte8.md`.

**The menu:** regular espresso drinks +$1 (flat white and cortado $6, cappuccino $7.50, latte and iced latte $7.50). Plain matcha stays $7, chai $6, Americano $4, signature drinks $8. The average of the 8 drinks on the founder's cost sheet is **$6.81**.

**The margin.** Each drink leaves **$3.40** (50% of the price) after ingredients and cup ($1.04), the 30% rent ($2.04) and Square's fee ($0.33). Fixed costs are about **$140 a month** (estimates), so break-even is **2 drinks a day**. At 60 a day the profit margin is **49%**. **Year 1: $55,780** operating profit on $115,089 of revenue.

**What that means for the two of you.** The profit is your pay. $55,780 is about **$27,890 each for the year**, or about **$14.10 an hour** for ~76 hours a week together, before income and self-employment tax. Still under NYC's $17 minimum wage. At the full 60 a day (from month 6), it's about $5,160 a month, or about $15.70 an hour.

**The line to watch: daily volume.**

| Drinks a day (at plan) | Year 1 profit |
|---|---|
| 60 (base) | $55,780 |
| 54 (−10%, the full +$1 menu's drop on the panel) | $50,034 |
| 48 (−20%) | $44,288 |
| 30 (−50%) | $27,050 |

This menu beats the old prices ($50,203) as long as you lose **fewer than about 10% of drinks**.

**Cash.** About **$1,529** before it pays for itself, inside your $2,000 budget. Earned back in month 1.

**Ways to improve it further:** negotiate rent to 25% (year 1 goes to $61,526, **+$5,746**, run through the tool), sell pastries with drinks (about +$6,500 a year at 15 a day, by hand), and push signature drinks at $8.

**Not included:** the signature drinks and the Americano (not on the cost sheet), pastries, sales tax (assumed added on top), income and self-employment tax. An accountant should check the structure, your mom's setup and tax. Not financial, tax or legal advice.
