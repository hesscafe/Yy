# Board verdict: Hess Cafe

**First meeting vote: 3 fund if, 0 pass. Average score: 4.7 / 10.** Second meeting: 3 fund if, average 5.7 (see below). (Offers 5, Monopoly 4, Product 5)

The board would back a cheap, time-limited test, not the business as pitched. Because rent is only a share of revenue, no utilities are paid and start-up costs are about $2,000, the downside is small. What isn't proven yet is whether customers will find the café, how fast it can serve them, and whether the deal with the bar protects the founder.

## Risks raised by more than one member (most serious first)

1. **The deal with the bar (all 3).** The term, notice period, exclusivity, equipment use, morning signage and permit cover are all unknown. The bar could end the deal or copy the menu once it is proven to work.
2. **Customers may not see it, and it may still look like a bar (all 3).** At 7am it looks like a closed bar on Christopher St. The interior can't change, signage outside may not be allowed, and the budget is $2,000.
3. **No reason for weekday commuters to choose it (all 3).** The signature drinks bring in weekend visitors, but nothing wins the 7:30am habit buyer. There is also no food.
4. **Slow service at peak hours (Offers, Product).** Layered lattes and meringue torched to order are slow, and there may only be one person working.
5. **Unknown cost per drink and equipment (all 3).** After a 25–30% revenue share and specialty ingredients, the margin is unknown. $2,000 won't buy an espresso machine and grinder if the bar has none.
6. **Easy to copy (Monopoly, Offers).** Recipes aren't a secret. Only a brand people seek out would protect the café.

## Update after the competitor research and buyer panel (2026-11-11)

**Competitors** (`competitors.md`): there is a real gap before 8:30am, because nobody nearby sells premium or dessert-flavoured matcha that early. At $7–8, Hess Cafe would be cheaper than Matchaful. The new biggest risk is the host itself: Mamali appears to run its own café in the mornings.

**Buyer panel** (`panel/results.md`, 20 simulated buyers, so treat the numbers as an upper bound):
- **12 buy · 8 pass (60%).** Buyers would come about 1.5–2 times in the first month (median 1.5), so it would start as an occasional stop, not a daily one.
- **Weekday commuters are the weak spot: 2 of 7 buy (29%).** They pass because of habit (they already have a café that knows their order) and trust (no reviews yet). West Village residents (5 of 6) and weekend visitors (3 of 5) buy.
- **Concerns buyers repeated:**
  1. Is the matcha good quality, or hidden under syrup?
  2. The $8 dessert lattes sound too sweet.
  3. It looks like a closed bar from the street.
  4. With no drip, there's no fast, cheap coffee.
- **Price answers (median):** a bargain at $5, getting expensive at $7.25, too expensive at $9. The $8 signature drinks sit above the "getting expensive" point for most buyers. The $5–7 regular drinks are fine.
- **What would turn a no into a yes:** naming the matcha's grade and origin, a free taste for commuters, a Google rating of 4.5 or higher, a sandwich board or obvious "open" sign, and posts on Instagram or TikTok.

**Drinks a day is still unknown.** The panel shows who buys and how often, but not how many people walk past. A 2-week test with a tally is the only reliable way to get the number. Until then, use the CFO scenarios: 40 drinks a day is about $3,200 a month and 80 is about $6,500 (6 days a week, drinks only, before income tax).

**Board conditions this changes:**
- Name the matcha's grade and origin on the menu. This is the most repeated reason buyers hesitate. *(`/founder-offer`)*
- Test the signature drinks at $7 as well as $8, and offer a less-sweet option. *(`/founder-pricing`)*
- The board's concern about weekday commuters is confirmed. Consider batch brew or an Americano at about $4 for speed, plus a punch card.

## Where the members disagree

- **Who the business is for.** The Offers lens wants a commuter offer added so weekdays pay. The Product lens warns that one person can't serve both commuters and the slow "show" drinks in the same hour, and would rather narrow the menu. The Monopoly lens would dominate the dessert-latte niche first and treat commuters as secondary.
- **The bar setting.** The Product lens sees "a pop-up café inside a bar" as a story worth leaning into. The Monopoly lens sees the host bar mainly as the biggest threat to the business.

## The strongest version the board can see

This is narrower than what was pitched. Start Hess Cafe as a clearly signed **morning pop-up inside Mamali**, not a full coffee shop. The menu would be two or three hero drinks, built around the torched-meringue moment, plus fast espresso and drip for commuters. Customers could pre-order, there would be a punch card, and a rotating drink of the week. Run it for 2–4 weeks with real prices before spending on décor, and only under a signed agreement that gives exclusivity in the morning and says the founder owns the recipes and brand. If the dessert drinks build a following, that brand can move to other bars that sit empty in the morning. If they don't, the loss stops at about $2,000.
