## What would flip a no

- A friend or a bunch of solid reviews saying the plain matcha is genuinely great and the line moves fast; then I'd grab the free sip and maybe switch on commute days.
- If my usual place closed or got slow, or if the plain matcha tasting was genuinely better than anything near me, I'd give the $8 matcha a real try.
- A plain matcha latte at $6 or less, or an early-commuter price on it, after the free sip shows me the matcha is actually good.
- If my usual café closed or was packed one morning, I'd try the $5 Americano or a free matcha sip on the way to the train. But that's a one-off, not a switch.
- A solid Google rating (4.5+ with a decent number of reviews) after a few weeks, or a free tasting sip on my way past that's actually good.
- A signature drink at $6 or under, or a small size at that price, so I could try one without feeling ripped off.
- Seeing a solid Google rating (4.5+ with a few dozen reviews) or a friend vouching for the matcha; the free tasting sip might get me to stop if it was clearly a café from the street.
- Seeing that strawberry cheesecake matcha in someone's hand or on Instagram, so I know it's a real café drink worth the detour.
- If my regular place closed or got slow, and this one could hand me a good plain latte or Americano in under two minutes, I'd switch.

Buyers say they would buy **2.5 times** in the first month on average.
