# Offers lens (round 2)
Score: 6/10 (was 5) | Vote: FUND IF (was FUND IF)

## What changed since the first meeting
- **The offer was rebuilt.** It is now "The 6am Matcha on Christopher St." It has a $7.50 signature drink with a half-sweet option, the matcha's grade and farm named on the menu, free tasting sips from 6 to 9am on weekdays, a $4 Americano, regular drinks made first during the rush, a free remake guarantee, a punch card (10th drink free) and a drink of the week (`offer.md`).
- **There is a margin per drink from the founder's cost sheet.** Each drink leaves about $3.07 on average after ingredients, the cup, 30% rent and Square's 2.6% + 15¢, with a range of $2.18 to $3.73. There are no fixed costs. The extra cost of the signature drinks is still an estimate ($0.50–1.00).
- **Competitors are mapped.** Matchaful, a block away, opens at 8:30am, and its matcha latte is listed at about $12.59 on a delivery app. No nearby shop was found selling dessert-flavoured matcha. Mamali itself appears to serve espresso and breakfast in the morning.
- **Simulated buyers moved.** In the re-test, 13 of 20 bought instead of 12, commuters went from 2 of 7 to 3 of 7, and first-month purchases rose from 24 to 31 (about 2.4 per buyer). The price curve caps the acceptable range at about $8 (`pricing-curve.md`).
- **Proof is now planned before any spending.** The first two weeks after opening (Nov 30 to Dec 12) are the test, with a pass line written in advance: 40+ drinks a day, 30+ on weekdays and 60+ on Saturdays. Décor money is held back until the test passes (`launch.md`).

## What is strong now
- **The stack targets real objections.** Each piece answers a reason buyers gave for passing. The named matcha and the free sip answer "is it good?", which was the most repeated doubt. Half-sweet answers "too sweet". The Americano and regular-drinks-first answer "I have a train to catch". The remake guarantee answers "no reviews". The offer costs about $5–7 a day to run, which is cheap for that much added belief.
- **The guarantee and loyalty are affordable.** Rent is a share of revenue, so a free drink costs only its ingredients, about $1. The guarantee and the punch card cost very little to keep.
- **The urgency is real.** The drink of the week really does change, and the opening double stamps really do end at 2 weeks. There are no fake "was $X" prices and no menu discounts.
- **Perceived likelihood rose from about 3 to about 6** on the value equation. That was the weakest of the four in the first meeting.
- **The downside is still small.** There is no fixed cost, the start-up budget is about $2,000, and spending past basic posters and a chalkboard waits until the test passes.

## What would still kill it (top 3)
1. **The host could take the proven offer.** Nothing is signed. Public listings say Mamali already serves morning coffee, and the deal has no exclusivity, term or notice period, and nothing saying who owns the recipes. The more the offer works, the easier it is for the host to keep the menu and the customers.
2. **The main proof isn't real yet.** "We put the farm on the menu" is the part of the stack that moved buyers most, but the supplier, grade and farm are not chosen. The matcha cost on the sheet ($0.39 a drink) has not been checked against a ceremonial-grade quote. If the grade the founder can afford isn't one worth naming, the free sip and the quality story lose their force. The signature drinks' real cost is also still a guess.
3. **The pay may not cover two people's hours, and weekdays are still weak.** Even with the new offer, only 3 of 7 commuters buy, and buyers come about 2.4 times a month, so it is an occasional treat rather than a daily habit. At the 40-drinks-a-day pass line, the cost sheet gives about $3,200 a month for roughly 300–350 hours a month across the founder and their mom. That is about $9–11 an hour before income tax, well under NYC's $17 minimum wage. The positioning also leans on "about $5 less than Matchaful", and this lens warns against winning on price. The price cannot be doubled, since buyers start calling it expensive around $7.50 and the acceptable range tops out at about $8.

## Conditions: which of my first conditions are met, partly met, or open
1. **The numbers close on paper: partly met.** The margin per drink is known from the cost sheet, and with no fixed costs there is no loss-making break-even point. Still open: the real matcha cost, the extra cost of the signature drinks, the real daily volume, and a target pay per hour for the two people.
2. **The deal and permits are in writing: open.** The bar provides the equipment and posters on the glass are allowed, but neither is in writing. Nothing is signed: term, notice, exclusivity, Mamali stopping its own morning coffee, liability, and ownership of the recipes and brand. It is not confirmed that the permit covers the founder operating at 70 Christopher St.
3. **A reason to come on weekdays: partly met.** The $4 Americano, regular drinks first, free sips and a punch card are in place. Pre-order is deferred. Simulated commuters went from 2 of 7 to 3 of 7, which is better but still the weakest group.
4. **Proof before scale: partly met.** The 2-week test, the pass lines, the $7.50 vs $8 price test and the décor hold are all planned. The test has not run.
5. **The offer has a name, a stack and a guarantee: met.** One gap: the named matcha has to be true before it is printed.

Not one of my conditions, but still open from the board's checklist: a shorter launch menu. The pitch still lists 5 signature drinks plus a torched hot chocolate, all made to order on a single espresso machine during the rush.
