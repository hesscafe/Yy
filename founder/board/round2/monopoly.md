# Monopoly lens (round 2)
Score: 5/10 (was 4) | Vote: FUND IF (was FUND IF)

The founder has done a lot of work, and it narrows the business toward a small market it could own. The competitor map found a real gap: nobody nearby serves premium or dessert-flavoured matcha before 8:30am. But the two things that would let Hess Cafe *hold* that gap have not moved. Nothing is signed with the host. And there is no single signature drink that customers would come back for. The new finding that Mamali may already sell morning coffee in the same space makes the host risk worse, not better.

## What changed since the first meeting
- **Competitors are mapped** (`competitors.md`). Matchaful (87 Christopher St, about a block away) opens at 8:30am with a matcha latte listed at about $12.59. Aokō opens at 11am. Chalait is closed. None of the listings found sell dessert-flavoured matcha.
- **A new threat came out of that research:** public listings describe Mamali itself as a café serving espresso and breakfast, one with a 7:30am opening. A CB2 filing from April 2025 recommended denying its wine licence.
- **Positioning is now narrower:** "Matcha from 6am" at the subway exit, for locals and weekend visitors first, with commuters as a secondary message. That is the small-market-first approach this lens asked for.
- **Distribution has a plan and a cost cap:** window and chalkboard, Instagram/TikTok, and Google reviews, with about $300 budgeted and a ceiling of about $5 to win a customer, worth about $7.50 in month one (`marketing.md`).
- **A test is defined:** the first 2 weeks after the Nov 30 opening are the test, with 40 drinks a day as the pass line, and no décor spend until it passes (`launch.md`).
- **Brand work is done:** keep "Hess Cafe · matcha from 6am", with the voice, colours and touchpoints set (`brand.md`).
- **The offer is built:** named matcha grade and farm, free tasting sips, half-sweet, a $4 Americano, a punch card, and a remake guarantee. The simulated panel went from 12 to 13 of 20.

## What is strong now
- **A small market with a clear edge in time:** from 6 to 8:30am, nobody nearby serves premium or dessert-flavoured matcha, and Hess Cafe's matcha is cheaper than Matchaful's. This is a real niche to dominate first.
- **A cost structure rivals can't easily match:** no fixed costs (rent is a revenue share, no utilities, equipment provided, no wages), so a slow week costs little and a cheap test is possible. About $3.07 is left per drink.
- **Distribution with a number on it:** a customer cap of about $5, cheap channels, and weekly checks (drinks a day, return rate, reviews). That answers part of the "first 100 customers" question, on paper.
- **The test comes before spending on décor,** so the downside stays at about $2,000.

## What would still kill it (top 3)
1. **The host can take it back, and may already compete.** No agreement is signed (term, notice, morning exclusivity, recipe and brand ownership). Mamali appears to run its own morning café in the same room. If Mamali keeps selling coffee in the morning, or ends the deal once the menu works, the founder has built value for the host. This is now the biggest risk, and it is open.
2. **The gap is hours, and hours can be copied.** The edge is "open before Matchaful" plus recipes. Matchaful is a block away and can open at 7am or add a cherry pie matcha within weeks. Nothing is close to 10x better, and there is still no secret. Only a brand people seek out would hold, and the brand still lists five signature drinks with no one drink to be known for.
3. **Weekday demand is unproven, and every number is simulated.** Commuters buy at 3 of 7 even after the new offer, buyers expect to come about 2.4 times in the first month (an occasional stop, not a habit), and drinks a day is unknown. If weekdays stay under 20 drinks, the niche is a weekend novelty, and that is not enough to hold a position.

## Conditions: which of my first conditions are met, partly met, or open
1. **A signed agreement** (12+ months, notice, morning exclusivity, founder owns recipes and brand, outside signage): **mostly open.** Only signage is settled (posters and a frame on the front glass are allowed; a sandwich board still needs Mamali's OK). Nothing is signed. The deadline is Nov 15, and the question of whether Mamali stops serving morning coffee is unanswered.
2. **Licences confirmed** (permit, inspection, liability): **partly met.** The founder holds a permit, but `ops.md` flags that it may not cover the founder operating at Mamali's address. Who carries liability and the insurance are both still open.
3. **A distribution test before spending on décor:** **partly met.** It is planned well: a 2-week test, a 40-drinks-a-day pass line, a $5 cost cap per customer, and décor held back. It has not run, so no channel's real cost per customer is known yet.
4. **A plan to build a brand around one signature drink:** **partly met.** The brand, tagline and touchpoints exist, but no single signature drink has been chosen, and the launch menu still has five.

## If FUND IF: the conditions still standing
1. **Sign the agreement with Mamali before opening:** a minimum term, written notice, a clause that Mamali does not sell coffee from 6am to 12pm, the founder owning the recipes and brand, and who repairs the equipment. If Mamali won't give up morning coffee, this lens passes.
2. **Confirm with DOHMH that the permit covers the founder at 70 Christopher St,** and put general liability insurance in place.
3. **Run the 2-week test as written and report the real numbers:** drinks a day split by weekday and Saturday, the return rate, reviews, and which channel brought each customer and what it cost. A pass is 40+ drinks a day.
4. **Pick one signature drink to own** (for example, the torched-meringue hot chocolate or the cherry pie matcha), lead with it everywhere, and cut the launch menu to two or three signatures. The aim is that "the place with X" becomes the brand, rather than "open earlier than Matchaful".
