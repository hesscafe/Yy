## What changed since the first meeting
- **The one sentence exists.** "Hess Cafe · Matcha from 6am" (`brand.md`), backed by a real gap: nobody nearby sells premium or dessert matcha before Matchaful opens at 8:30am (`competitors.md`). That answers my first question better than the original pitch did.
- **The first five minutes are designed.** Window chalkboard ("Open for coffee now"), posters with prices, a removable décor box (runner, pennants, stickers on plain cups), a punch card and review QR at the counter, a scripted greeting (`ops.md`, `brand.md`). Front-glass signage is confirmed; the sandwich board is not.
- **Staffing and the line have a plan.** Founder plus mom on the 6–9am rush, one on the machine and one on the till. Regular drinks first, signature drinks after. Target under 2 minutes for a regular, 3–4 for a signature. A rule for a line of more than 6, and a fallback that pauses signature drinks 7:30–9:00.
- **Unit cost is mostly known.** From the founder's cost sheet: about $3.07 left per drink on average after ingredients, cup, 30% rent and the card fee ($2.18–$3.73). The signature extras are still an estimate ($0.50–1.00), and so is matcha (no supplier quote yet).
- **A real test is scheduled.** Dry run Nov 24, soft open Nov 28, opening Nov 30. The first 2 weeks are the test, with a pass line of 40 drinks a day, written down before opening.
- **The menu got bigger, not smaller.** Still five signature drinks plus the torched hot chocolate, plain matcha, chai, the full espresso menu, a new $4 Americano, $6 pastries, a drink of the week and free tasting sips during the rush.

## What is strong now
- **Matcha is the spine.** Naming the grade and farm, the free sip and half-sweet all point at one thing: a good matcha, early. The re-test moved matcha fans and commuters (12 → 13 of 20 simulated buyers, commuters 2 → 3 of 7). Simulated, so an upper bound.
- **Owning the touchpoints that can be owned.** Window, cup, punch card, first post and the reply to the first complaint are all specified, cheap and in the brand colours. The remake guarantee closes the "bad drink" seam.
- **Operating detail shows care for the parts nobody sees.** Fridge temperature at opening, test shots tasted, backflush and closing routine, waste and tally every day, a backup torch, a risk register with a plan per failure.
- **Learning before spending.** Décor money is held back until the test passes, and the plan says to trust real counts over the panel.

## What would still kill it (top 3)
1. **Too many things in one 6–9am hour.** Two people must make espresso drinks, a $4 Americano, layered dessert matcha, torched meringue, pour free sips, sell pastries, stamp cards and ask for reviews, all at one machine they have not timed yet. With no drip, every coffee goes through the espresso machine. If the commuter waits 5 minutes on day 3, the "subway exit" promise breaks and the weak segment (3 of 7) is gone. The menu cut I asked for was not made; the offer added work instead.
2. **The seams with Mamali are still unsigned.** No written agreement, no confirmed exclusivity (public listings suggest Mamali serves its own morning coffee), equipment condition and repairs not in writing, and the permit may cover Mamali rather than the founder (`ops.md`). A customer who meets Mamali's coffee, a broken machine or a closed door at 7am gets a broken experience the founder cannot fix.
3. **The quality promise is printed before the product is.** The brand promise and hooks say "we put the farm on the menu", but the matcha supplier, grade and farm are unknown and no quote is in. The signature recipes are not costed. If the matcha that arrives is ordinary, the whole positioning ("a matcha worth the walk") rests on syrup, which is the exact doubt buyers raised most.
