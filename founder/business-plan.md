## The idea

- What it is: a morning-only café (6am–12pm) run inside an existing bar, Mamali, at 70 Christopher St, West Village, New York City, with a funky, cute look.
- Who it is for: a mix of West Village locals and commuters on weekdays, and visitors / Instagram-driven customers (tourists) on weekends.
- What it sells, at what price: signature dessert-inspired lattes at $7–8 each — matcha latte, strawberry cheesecake matcha latte, cherry pie matcha latte, halva latte, apple strudel latte — plus regular coffee drinks (espresso-based; no drip), a hot chocolate topped with torched (burnt) meringue, and pastries.
- Where and how: walk-in café in the Mamali bar space, mornings only, before the bar opens. Customers find it by foot traffic (a frame and posters on the front glass) and social media.
- Budget and constraints: start-up budget about $2,000. Cannot change the interior of the bar (any design must be non-permanent / removable). Rent is 25–30% of revenue (a revenue share), with no utilities to pay. The bar provides the equipment. The founder holds the permit.

## Summary

**What it is.** A morning-only café (6am–12pm, 6 days a week) inside the Mamali bar at 70 Christopher St, at the Christopher St–Stonewall subway exit in the West Village. It sells dessert-flavoured matcha lattes, torched-meringue hot chocolate, regular espresso drinks and Balthazar pastries to West Village locals, weekend visitors and 1-train commuters.

**Verdict: Profitable, on a guessed 60 drinks a day.** The verdict is computed, not written by hand, and all four checks pass. It rests on the founder's estimate of 60 drinks a day, which the 2-week test (Nov 30 – Dec 12) has to confirm.

**The menu (main plan):** regular espresso drinks +$1 (flat white and cortado $6, cappuccino and latte $7.50), plain matcha $7, chai $6, Americano $4, signature drinks $8, pastries $6. See `pricing.md`.

**The three numbers** (from `cfo.md`):
- **Margin:** each drink leaves **$3.40** (50%). The profit margin at 60 a day is **49%**.
- **Break-even:** **2 drinks a day.** Fixed costs are only about $140 a month: no fixed rent, no utilities, no wages.
- **Year 1:** **$55,780** operating profit on $115,089 of revenue, ramping from 30 to 60 drinks a day over 6 months. With 10% fewer drinks it's $50,034. At half the volume, $27,050.

**But it's your pay, not profit on top of pay.** $55,780 is about $27,890 each for you and your mom, or about **$14.10 an hour** before tax for ~76 hours a week together. That's still below NYC's $17 minimum wage. Rent at 25% would add $5,746 a year, and pastries about $6,500.

**The board (second review): 3 fund if, average 5.7 / 10** (up from 4.7). All three ask for the same things first: a signed deal with Mamali, a real named matcha, and a launch menu cut to 2 signature drinks.

**Where the board and the buyers agree:** the risks are the deal with Mamali, being seen at 7am, and winning weekday commuters (2–3 of 7 commuters buy). The drinks themselves are a real draw for locals and weekend visitors. **Where they disagree:** the board wants a short launch menu; the pitch still has 5 signature drinks. Buyers worry most about matcha quality, which the board barely raised.

**Pay per hour:** at 40 drinks a day, about $3,200 a month for two people's ~300–350 hours is about $9–11 an hour before tax. Set the hourly pay that makes it worth it for you, and add it to the test.

**Biggest risk:** Mamali itself. Public listings describe it as a café serving espresso drinks in the morning, and nothing is signed yet. **Plan:** a written agreement before Nov 15, with a term, notice period, morning exclusivity, and the recipes and brand staying yours.

**What you need to start:** about **$1,529** (CFO), inside your $2,000 budget: about $300 for marketing (posters, chalkboard, punch cards, free drinks), first stock of matcha, beans, milk, cups and syrups, a torch, a Square reader, and fees for insurance and registration. No large spend before the 2-week test passes (40+ drinks a day).

**The one thing to do this week:** get the agreement with Mamali in writing, including that Mamali stops serving coffee 6am–12pm. If Mamali won't agree to that, one board member votes pass.

### 4. Routines (one page each)

**Opening (5:15–6:00)**
1. Fridge under 41°F. Milk dates okay.
2. Machine on. Grind and pull 2 test shots. Taste one.
3. Sift matcha, fill syrups, prep meringue, fill the tasting cups.
4. Décor box out. Chalkboard written. Card reader on and test a $0.01 charge.
5. At 6:00, unlock and put the chalkboard in the window or doorway.

**Serving the rush**
1. Greet. Offer a tasting sip to anyone who hesitates.
2. Take the order and payment first. Ask "half-sweet?" for signature drinks.
3. Make regular drinks and Americanos first. Signature drinks next, the torched hot chocolate last.
4. Call the name, stamp the punch card, point to the review QR card.
5. If the line is longer than 6 people, the second person only takes orders and makes Americanos.

**Complaint**
1. Listen. Say sorry.
2. Remake it free, no arguing (that's the guarantee).
3. Write it in the log: what drink, what was wrong.
4. If it's online, reply within a day (see `brand.md`).

**Closing (12:00–12:45)**
1. Chalkboard in. Last orders done.
2. Backflush and wipe the machine. Clean steam wands, jugs, blender.
3. Throw away opened milk past its time. Count waste.
4. Pack the décor box. Leave the bar exactly how Mamali wants it.
5. Write the tally: drinks by type, pastries sold and wasted, card + cash total.

**Weekly reorder (every Saturday)**
1. Count matcha, beans, milk, cups, lids, syrups, butane.
2. Anything under 1 week of stock: reorder.
3. Adjust the Balthazar pastry order: sold out by 10am → order 2 more; more than 2 left at noon → order 2 fewer.

### 6. Licences, permits and insurance (NYC)

Rules differ by place and change. **Confirm each with the authority or a professional. Not legal advice.**

- [ ] **Food service permit valid for this address and operation.** You said you have a permit. Check it covers *you operating at 70 Christopher St* in the morning, not just Mamali's operation. NYC rules generally require each operator to hold their own permit. One route is to operate under the host restaurant's permit with its supervision ([NYC Business: Food Service Establishment Permit](https://nyc-business.nyc.gov/nycbusiness/description/food-service-establishment-permit), [Shared Kitchens](https://nyc-business.nyc.gov/nycbusiness/description/shared-kitchens)). Ask DOHMH which one applies.
- [ ] **Food Protection Certificate:** a certified food protection supervisor must be on site during operation (confirm with DOHMH).
- [ ] **Business registration:** an LLC or a DBA ("Hess Cafe") with New York State / the County Clerk.
- [ ] **Sales tax Certificate of Authority** from [NY State Tax](https://www.tax.ny.gov/bus/st/register.htm) before your first sale. NYC sales tax is 8.875%.
- [ ] **EIN** from the IRS (free) for a bank account.
- [ ] **General liability insurance**, with Mamali possibly named as additional insured. Ask Mamali what they require.
- [ ] **Sidewalk sign:** per [NYC 311](https://portal.311.nyc.gov/article/?kanumber=KA-01980), a sandwich board can extend no more than 3 ft from the building and be no more than 5 ft high. Confirm with 311 or DOT, and get Mamali's OK.
