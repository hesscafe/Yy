# Rent options: Hess Cafe

All at the current menu (average $6.81 a drink), the guessed 60 drinks a day, a ramp from 30 to 60 over 6 months, 26 days a month, and $140/month of other fixed costs. Year 1 at the plan comes from the CFO tool (`cfo.md`, `cfo-rent25.md`, `cfo-rent-flat4000.md`). The lower-volume rows are calculated the same way by hand.

| Year 1 profit | 30% of sales (now) | **25% of sales** | **Flat $4,000/month** |
|---|---|---|---|
| 60 a day (plan) | $55,780 | **$61,526** | **$42,256** |
| 54 a day (−10%) | $50,034 | $55,212 | $33,107 |

| Break-even | 2 drinks a day | 2 drinks a day | **30 drinks a day** |
| Month 1 (30 a day) | $2,512 profit | $2,778 | **about $100** |

## What it means

- **25% of sales is the best deal at any volume.** It's $5,746 more than today at 60 a day, and you still can't lose money on a slow month.
- **A flat $4,000 is worse unless you sell a lot.** It only beats 30% above about **75 drinks a day**, and only beats 25% above about **90 a day**. At your 60-a-day guess it costs you about **$13,500 a year** compared with 30%, and **$19,300** compared with 25%. You'd also need 30 drinks a day just to break even, so a slow first month could cost you money.
- **"$4,000 or 25%, whichever is higher"** (a common lease deal) works out the same as a flat $4,000 for you. 25% of sales only goes over $4,000 when sales pass $16,000 a month, which is about 90 drinks a day. At 60 a day you'd always pay the $4,000.

## Recommendation

Ask Mamali for **25% of sales, with no minimum**. If they insist on a minimum, keep it low (for example, $1,500–2,000 a month) and start it only after the 2-week test, once you know your real volume. Don't sign a flat $4,000 or a $4,000 minimum before you've seen real numbers.
